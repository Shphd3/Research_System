# Prior-Art Mechanism Decision

Use when literature can change a nontrivial mechanism's selection, adaptation or
admissibility. A prescribed deterministic operation needs no literature ceremony.
Clarify the question, mathematical constraints and supervision boundary first.

Search the smallest decision-changing question. Prefer primary sources, inspect
negative results and limits, and stop when a falsifiable baseline and material
alternatives are understood. Lack of precedent is not evidence of novelty.

For each retained representative explain, in ordinary words:
1. Problem: actual input, output and question.
2. How: what is measured, constructed or updated and how it produces the output.
3. Why: the structural assumption that makes this computation useful.
4. Evidence and limits: demonstrated result, assumptions, cost and counterexample.
5. Supervision and compatibility: source of targets and any forbidden information.
6. Decision: adopt, adapt, baseline, external-only, reject or defer, with reason.

A method name, generic advantage or “self-supervised” label is not an explanation.
Trace hidden teachers in pseudo-targets, pretraining, labels/identity, simulation,
future observations, rewards, filtering and checkpoint/operating-point selection.
Evaluation-only fields cannot leak into training, candidates, fitting or decisions.
Privileged upper bounds must remain explicitly separate.

`scholar-mode` supplies source-grounded analysis; the Anchor's section 5 owns the
selected design decision beside its computation. Do not add a competing design,
mandatory literature catalogue or new top-level Anchor section. A missing source
or supervision conflict remains unresolved rather than silently repaired.
