## 1. Paper Summary

This paper investigates numerical precision placement in differentiable Gram matrix solvers, focusing on whether close agreement in forward predictions implies equivalence in backward gradients and parameter updates.

The authors partition the solver into three computational stages—statistic construction, Cholesky factorization, and solution-side computation—assigning either FP32 or FP64 precision to each stage. Using a controlled Spectral Koopman Attention (SKA) model and an independent episodic ridge regression classifier, they compare forward predictions, full-model gradients, optimizer updates, and separately trained model endpoints across different precision routes.

Across $18$ matched SKA validation comparisons from three training seeds, comparing the mixed-precision route $q_{110}$ against the higher-precision solver reference route $q_{111}$ yields median relative discrepancies of $0.962\%$ in logits, $8.61\%$ in full-model gradients, and $9.88\%$ in restored AdamW updates, each normalized by its corresponding $q_{111}$ reference norm.

These results demonstrate that measurable discrepancies in gradients and parameter updates can persist even when forward outputs are close. Furthermore, local update sensitivity is influenced by specific computational stages and algebraically equivalent dual reformulations (evaluated at a single fixed checkpoint), whereas in the tested classifier setting, post-training conditioning separation strongly depends on the training-time ridge regularization policy. The separately trained endpoints exhibit regime-dependent behavior, and the experiments do not demonstrate that improvements in numerical conditioning lead to task performance gains.

Overall, the primary contribution of this paper is the insight that forward prediction similarity does not adequately reflect discrepancies in actual optimizer updates, while demonstrating that stagewise precision and solver implementation choices shape local update divergences and that training-time regularization policies strongly affect post-training conditioning separation.

## 3. Critical Strengths

**Critical Strengths:**
* **Stagewise Variable Control and Multi-Aspect Empirical Comparisons:** The paper decomposes the differentiable Gram matrix solver into three computational stages—statistic construction, Cholesky factorization, and solution-side computation—and systematically assigns FP32 or FP64 precision to each stage to construct controlled comparison routes. 
At fixed model states, the study combines matched route comparisons (measuring discrepancies in forward outputs, full-model gradients, and restored AdamW updates) with custom backward-pass (VJP) replacement diagnostics (§4.3, Table 5) and primal versus algebraically equivalent dual reformulation comparisons (§4.4, Table 7). At the training-endpoint level, it further pairs separately trained endpoint evaluations with targeted ridge regularization interventions (§4.4, Table 6; §4.5, Figure 1; §4.6, Table 8).
Within the scope of each experimental setup, the empirical observations are directly supported by the controlled comparisons.

## 4. Critical Weaknesses

**Critical Weaknesses:**

* **Weakness 1: The Conceptual Increment Beyond Established Numerical Sensitivity Needs Further Clarification.** From a numerical analysis perspective, the fact that close agreement in forward function evaluations does not imply closeness in derivatives is a known property, and the authors note in §2 and §5 that the numerical sensitivity of linear solvers and their derivatives has been examined in prior literature. While §4.2–4.3 extend this analysis by quantifying discrepancies in full-model gradients and restored AdamW updates across solver stages, the paper does not fully clarify what new training insights this quantification provides beyond existing knowledge of derivative sensitivity. Although the matched optimizer-update measurements and common-route endpoint evaluations provide empirical information beyond the general observation of derivative sensitivity, the paper offers limited guidance on when the measured discrepancies are consequential enough to change a practical precision choice. This limits the practical significance of the contribution in my assessment.
* **Weakness 2: Insufficient Mechanistic Links Connecting the Separate Empirical Observations.** The paper appropriately separates its local and endpoint findings and does not claim a demonstrated causal chain across training or a task-performance benefit. My concern is therefore about the explanatory reach and significance of the contribution, rather than the validity of its stated empirical findings. Specifically, although the paper conducts targeted diagnostic and interventional analyses across multiple setups, the explanatory connections linking these individual findings remain underdeveloped:
  1. *Link between local update discrepancies and separately trained endpoints (§4.3–4.4):* Section 4.3 (Table 5) shows across 29 fixed state–sample units that custom backward (VJP) solver computations affect local update consistency, and §4.4 (Table 7) examines primal versus dual reformulations at a fixed checkpoint; meanwhile, §4.4 (Table 6) shows that separately trained endpoints across different precision routes exhibit differences in summary statistics. However, the paper does not examine how these local step-wise update discrepancies propagate or accumulate during training to produce the separately trained endpoint differences in Table 6.
  2. *Practical training implications of endpoint conditioning separation (§4.5–4.6):* Sections 4.5–4.6 demonstrate that, in the tested classifier setting, post-training conditioning separation depends on the training-time ridge regularization policy. Yet, the paper does not establish how these conditioning differences explain broader training behavior or guide precision configuration in practice, particularly given that the experiments do not demonstrate task performance gains from improved conditioning.

## 5. Questions For Authors

1. **Substantive Training Insights Beyond Known Derivative Sensitivity (§2, §4.2–4.3, §5):** Given that the numerical sensitivity of linear solvers and their derivatives is established in prior literature, what previously unknown, substantive insights into model training do the quantified discrepancies in full-model gradients and restored AdamW updates establish, and how can they inform practical precision or training decisions?

2. **Mechanistic Links Between Local Discrepancies and Training Endpoints (§4.2–4.6):** The paper separately reports local update discrepancies at fixed states (§4.2–4.3; §4.4, Table 7), differences across separately trained endpoints (§4.4, Table 6), and regularization-dependent conditioning separation (§4.5–4.6). Could the authors address two related questions: (a) Do the existing analyses support any connection between the local update discrepancies and the endpoint differences? If not, please clarify what conclusions about training outcomes can and cannot be drawn from the local diagnostics; and (b) What practical training significance do the observed conditioning differences hold without established task-performance benefits (§4.6, Table 8)?

**Minor:**
1. **Generalizability Beyond Strictly Controlled Architectures (§3.2):** Given the tightly controlled experimental setups (e.g., the simplified SKA architecture in §3.2), to what extent do the authors expect these findings and sensitivity patterns to hold when key architectural or experimental constraints are relaxed?
2. **Presentation and Experimental Organization:** While the technical reporting is detailed, the experimental protocols, evaluation cohorts (e.g., the 9-unit, 18-unit, 20-batch, 29-unit, and 36-unit setups), and extensive cross-references to Appendices A–C are somewhat fragmented across sections, making the main text dense to follow. Streamlining the presentation of these cohorts would improve readability.
