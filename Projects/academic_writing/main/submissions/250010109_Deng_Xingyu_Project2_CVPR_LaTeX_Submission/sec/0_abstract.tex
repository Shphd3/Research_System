\begin{abstract}
We study when an unconstrained top-1 mixture-of-experts router naturally uses
experts uniformly. In a controlled setting, the answer is geometric: if
route-relevant token directions are uniform on the sphere and expert prototypes
induce equal-measure cells, then expected expert usage is uniform. This
sufficient condition separates natural balance from finite-token sampling noise
and from capacity-constrained assignment, which can force equal counts without
explaining the representation. We also clarify the role of spectral evidence:
covariance isotropy is only a second-order diagnostic and does not imply
spherical directional uniformity, while whitening recovers the spherical model
in a controlled elliptical family. Spectral expert directions, including router
rows produced by power-iteration-style updates, are candidate centers rather
than balance guarantees. Synthetic experiments mirror these claims and make no
claim about semantic expert identity, downstream utility, or training-time
preservation.
\end{abstract}
