# Clear research writing and useful media

[中文](report_writing_cn.md)

Help the reader understand the question, reasoning, evidence and boundary on the
first reading. Use these rules when writing a report or explaining research;
they do not create another review stage or change scientific authority.

## Language

- Prefer short sentences with an explicit subject and a concrete verb. Separate
  an observation, its interpretation and its consequence when one sentence hides
  their relationship. Use active voice when the actor is known.
- Use one consistent term for each object. Define necessary technical terms and
  symbols where they first matter. Do not vary synonyms for stylistic effect.
- Put the main answer early. Each paragraph develops one point; a link supplies
  evidence rather than substituting for the explanation.
- Keep assumptions, quantifiers, uncertainty and scope. Simpler wording must not
  turn a local observation into a general claim, a hypothesis into a result, or
  a mathematical relation into a causal finding.
- Write natural Chinese and clear English. Do not enforce English word counts
  on Chinese, impose a controlled dictionary on scientific notation, or claim
  ASD-STE100 compliance without a separate compliance check.

## Content and depth

Start from the reader's question. Explain the minimum chain that connects the
objects, argument or comparison, evidence, bounded answer and remaining decision.
For a meeting, put the speaking core first and details/Q&A below it. For a full
report, supply enough explanation to stand alone. A short answer needs no report.

Use headings suited to the research. Formal arguments, observational studies,
interventions and engineering comparisons need different evidence; none requires
an invented experiment or model to fill a template. The existing eight-section
Anchor and four-part Summary keep their owning contracts.

## Choose the medium for a specific benefit

| Reader need | Useful form |
|---|---|
| A direct answer, stable record or citation | Text/Markdown |
| Relations, computation or dependencies | A small labeled diagram |
| Measured comparisons or uncertainty | A scientific figure with provenance |
| Explore a parameter, switch conditions, reveal a derivation or search a long report | Local HTML with a static reading fallback |
| Explain a time-dependent process to a defined audience | An animation/video when requested or clearly useful |

HTML is a derived explanation, not another editable source of findings. Reuse
the same source documents and verified data; link them and record their versions.
Label illustrative simulations and keep them separate from measurements. Preserve
units, ranges, uncertainty and all compared conditions when adding controls.

Do not build a website for an ordinary answer. Do not require a service, account,
network connection or video for reading the core result. Publishing and external
sync retain their explicit scope. A local reading bundle is not publication.

## Check the delivered experience

Check that a new reader can state what was asked, why the argument/comparison
answers it, what changed, and what remains unresolved. Without an independent
reader, call this self-review. Inspect actual rendered figures/pages when possible;
check navigation, labels, source links and a readable fallback. Source checks do
not establish rendered correctness or better comprehension.

This adapts the clear-language and medium-selection ideas in
[Karpathy's post](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2105819303471976479), rather than
adopting a new report template. The [STE maintenance group's FAQ](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html)
supports applying short sentences, clear terms and active voice beyond maintenance
documents. The post's informal "80%" is not a compliance level.
