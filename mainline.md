# Mainline: Feature-Zipf Hierarchical MoE

Last updated: 2026-05-07

## Long-Term Vision

Design MoE architectures that can learn quickly and avoid forgetting by aligning experts with sparse, hierarchical, Zipfian mechanisms in language.

## Current Research Question

What is the right definition and mechanism of MoE specialization?

More concretely: should specialization be measured at the feature level, the function level, the routing level, or by continual-learning interference?

## Background Assumptions

1. Natural language contains sparse features whose activation frequencies are highly uneven.
2. Some semantic or functional mechanisms may have hierarchical structure.
3. Expert specialization should mean more than routing frequency. It may involve feature selectivity, function alignment, routing stability, and tail generalization.
4. Synthetic data is useful only when it isolates a mechanism that is hard to observe directly in real hidden states.

## Core Hypothesis H1

If hidden representations are generated by hierarchical low-dimensional mechanisms, and fine-grained semantic mechanisms follow a Zipfian long tail, then a suitable Hierarchical MoE should:

1. route different fine-grained mechanisms to different experts;
2. align expert functions with true mechanisms;
3. show stronger specialization than random routing and flat MoE controls.

## Current Safest Claim

On feature-Zipf synthetic data, Hierarchical MoE recovers part of the hierarchical computation mechanism, especially in function recovery. However, the evidence does not yet support the stronger claim that Hierarchical MoE is better than flat middle spectral MoE at sparse feature division.

## Current Claim Boundary

Can say:

- Feature-Zipf synthetic data is a better controlled setting than directly making leaf labels Zipfian.
- The mechanism is recoverable under oracle routing.
- Learned routers exploit some sparse feature signal beyond random routing.
- Hierarchical MoE gives a function-level recovery signal in the variable-A setting.

Cannot yet say:

- Hierarchical MoE clearly beats flat MoE at sparse feature specialization.
- Learned hierarchy recovers the true leaf mechanism.
- Synthetic specialization already transfers to real language hidden states.

## Open Questions

1. Is the most important specialization feature-level or function-level?
2. Why does middle spectral routing help sparse feature division?
3. What architecture makes hierarchy useful beyond oracle settings?
4. How can synthetic diagnostics transfer to real language hidden states?
5. Which metrics best predict continual-learning benefit rather than only synthetic recoverability?

## Next Decisive Direction

Diagnose why flat middle spectral routing is currently strongest on sparse feature division, and separate feature-level specialization from function-level specialization in the next experiment.
