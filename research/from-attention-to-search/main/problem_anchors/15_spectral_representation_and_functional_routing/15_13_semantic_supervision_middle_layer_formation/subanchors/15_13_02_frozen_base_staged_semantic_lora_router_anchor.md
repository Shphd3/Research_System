# A15_13_02: Can Frozen Base-Model Semantics Seed a Staged Coarse-to-Fine LoRA Router?


## 1. Problem Definition

The decision question is whether semantic partitions obtained directly from a frozen Qwen3-0.6B-Base model can supervise a sequence-level Router that first learns ten coarse natural-language domains and then, after the coarse Router is fixed, learns fifteen fine intents inside each domain through newly added low-rank experts. The local object is the effective representation created by those low-rank residuals at one middle-late Transformer block; the original base-model parameters remain frozen.

The smallest blocking uncertainty is whether the staged mechanism works without first post-training a separate teacher. A Pass admits a more realistic domain--intent--slot dataset. A Fail with a working gold-label upper bound localizes the bottleneck to semantic discovery or signal transfer. A failed gold-label upper bound stops interpretation of clustering and returns the route to architecture or objective diagnosis. Incomplete labels, collapsed routes, invalid freezes, missing paired runs, or an absent functional-utility gate yield Insufficient evidence.

## 2. Physical Priors

The human-confirmed prior is that natural-language intents share domain-level meaning before differing in local intent-level meaning. A staged system may therefore preserve a learned coarse dispatch rule and allocate new capacity to distinctions left inside each coarse partition. This prior is exposed by held-out routing structure and within-domain fine-intent accessibility. It is weakened for this realization if a compute-matched flat Router or an otherwise identical hierarchy without semantic Router supervision matches or exceeds the staged system.

## 3. Falsifiable Hypotheses

The primary hypothesis predicts that a gold-label hierarchy first establishes an attainable upper bound and that at least one frozen-base clustering method recovers a positive fraction of that upper bound relative to the task-only hierarchy. Recovery must occur in the same direction for held-out fine-intent representation accessibility and task performance, while route entropy remains non-collapsed and expert deletion has category-selective effects.

The strongest rival is generic adaptation: shared and expert LoRA parameters improve the classifier, while the semantic labels merely make Router outputs look organized. Under that rival, Dense, flat, task-only, or shuffled-label controls match the representation and task changes, or expert deletion lacks category selectivity even when route-label agreement is high.

## 4. Mathematical Model

For utterance \(x\), let \(h(x)\) be the last non-padding hidden state entering the fifteenth Transformer block of the frozen base model. A coarse Router produces \(p_c(c\mid h)\) and selects one parent expert. After coarse training, its parameters are fixed. A child Router inside the selected parent produces \(p_f(f\mid c,h_r)\), where \(h_r=h\) for native routing or

\[
h_r=(I-P_c)h
\]

for coarse-direction removal. Here \(P_c\) is the regularized orthogonal projector onto the centered row space of the frozen coarse Router. The projection changes only the child Router input; all low-rank experts receive the native layer activation.

At one MLP linear map, the active transformation is the frozen weight plus one shared low-rank update, one selected parent update, and one selected child update. Task cross entropy updates the classification head and active low-rank experts. Route cross entropy updates only the active Router. Frozen-base cluster labels or gold labels supply route targets; no separately trained teacher supplies labels.

## 5. Computational Realization

CLINC150 provides English natural-language requests with 10 balanced domains and 150 balanced intents, 15 intents per domain. One example is an utterance whose required task output is one fine-intent class. Training labels fit the task head and the gold upper bound. Unsupervised route labels are constructed only from training-split final-layer states of the untouched base model. Development data select checkpoints. Confirmation labels remain sealed until models and rules freeze.

The base model is frozen throughout. Stage one trains a ten-way coarse Router, shared LoRA, parent LoRA experts, and the common intent head. Stage two fixes the coarse Router, adds one fifteen-way child Router and fifteen child LoRA experts under each parent, and either keeps parent experts trainable or freezes them. The child Router either reads the native state or the state with the coarse Router row space removed, forming the registered \(2\times2\) mechanism audit.

Gold domain and intent labels define the upper bound. Frozen-base pseudo-labels are generated by spherical K-means, diagonal Gaussian mixtures, or a shared-concentration von Mises--Fisher mixture, each first at ten coarse clusters and then at fifteen child clusters within every coarse cluster. A flat 150-way system, a hierarchy without route supervision, a dense LoRA continuation, and frequency-matched shuffled labels separate topology, capacity, task gradients, and label geometry.

## 6. Minimal Falsification Tests

The decisive quantity is the Oracle-relative recovery of held-out within-domain fine-intent linear-probe macro F1 from the effective fifteenth-block representation:

\[
R_{\mathrm{probe}}=
\frac{M_{\mathrm{pseudo}}-M_{\mathrm{task-only}}}
{M_{\mathrm{oracle}}-M_{\mathrm{task-only}}}.
\]

\(M\) is the equally weighted mean of ten domain-local 15-intent test macro-F1 scores, in percentage points. The ratio is admissible only when the gold hierarchy improves over the task-only hierarchy in the registered direction with a non-negligible denominator. Positive recovery in both paired seeds and a positive pooled interval support transfer from frozen-base semantics. A non-positive self-supervised effect with a valid gold upper bound is a Fail for the semantic entrance. A non-positive or inconsistent gold effect makes the self-supervised claim Insufficient for this architecture.

Routing entropy is a central mechanism analysis: per-example entropy measures decisiveness, dataset load entropy detects concentration, and gold-conditional route entropy measures semantic consistency. None is monotonic by itself, so each is compared directly with the Oracle and accompanied by route-label mutual information. Category-selective expert deletion is a hard functional eligibility gate: without a positive matched-category deletion effect in both seeds, the method is recorded as not functionally working even if its primary representation score improves.

## 7. Current Evidence

The previous CLINC150 E03 trained a separate teacher and compared its backward hierarchy with a flat semantic Router. The paired representation effect was near zero with opposite seed directions, while the gold hierarchy was only numerically positive; therefore the exact learned-teacher chain was not established. That result leaves open the present question because the current design removes teacher post-training, uses route-only semantic supervision, implements actual low-rank experts, completes the \(2\times2\) freeze/removal audit, and treats expert utility as a gate rather than inferring it from geometry.

No result exists yet for this frozen-base staged LoRA realization. The official balanced hierarchy and the verified local CLINC150 snapshot make the experiment constructible; they do not establish that base-model clusters are semantic.

## 8. Claim Boundary And Next Decision

A Pass would support only that, on balanced English intent classification with a frozen 0.6B base, one named staged LoRA Router can convert a frozen-base semantic partition into Oracle-directed routing and effective-representation changes with category-selective expert utility. It cannot establish open-domain semantic discovery, original-base representation formation, generative-task benefit, efficiency superiority, dynamic label refreshing, or transfer to token-level slots.

Exactly one next decision follows the complete run: decide whether the next experiment should extend the supported mechanism to MASSIVE or return to a controlled synthetic diagnosis. Completion requires all registered baselines, both paired seeds, the full \(2\times2\) audit, task/routing/representation/function tables, audited figures, and a typed Pass, Fail, or Insufficient verdict.
