## Purpose, hypothesis and controls

On frozen Qwen3-0.6B Base representations, test whether fixed routing constructed from feature-frequency intervals improves next-token prediction (NTP) learning on inputs containing low-frequency features, relative to random routing that preserves group sizes. NTP predicts the actual next token from the preceding text already visible at the current position. Its loss is natural-log cross-entropy per valid prediction position. The hypothesis to test is that A has lower low-frequency validation loss than B. Rival explanations include induced load and repeated sample exposure, score scale, branch optimization and overfitting. This experiment does not validate φ grouping, judge capacity in pretraining from scratch, or use a flat spectrum as a success criterion.

| Condition | Experts and routing | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| A | Eight independent ordinary FFNs; eight equal-width TRAIN-frequency intervals on the original probability scale; fixed top-1 routing with a unit gate | NTP |
| B | Randomly regroup the same feature pool, preserving A's feature count in every group exactly; do not match within-group frequency; use the same form of fixed top-1 routing and unit gate | NTP |
| C | The same eight experts; a trainable linear router on normalized h; multiply the selected expert output by its selected softmax probability | NTP |
| D | Identical to C | NTP + 0.01 LB |

A/B is the primary comparison. C/D tests only the effect of load balancing (LB) within this trainable-router setting. A/D is only a comparison of complete methods. The selected-probability gate in C/D lets task gradients reach the router. An argmax followed by a non-differentiable unit gate must not be mistaken for a router that learns from the task.

## Grouping, scores and testability

Compute all frequencies and eligibility from valid prediction positions in the new TRAIN cache only. A hit means z_i > 0. The frequency p_i is feature i's hit count n_i divided by N_train, the number of valid TRAIN prediction positions. The eligible pool requires n_i ≥ 200, coverage of at least 20 original 1,024-token contexts, and n_i < N_train. Apply `np.linspace` to the pool's [p_min, p_max] on the **original probability scale** to create eight equal-width intervals. Intervals are left-closed and right-open, except that the final interval includes p_max. Do not bin by feature counts or log frequency.

A preliminary read-only inspection of the old event cache gave reference group counts `[9253,183,124,34,7,3,2,9]`. Freeze the actual counts from TRAIN after the same-window supplementary extraction. Small bins or poor assignments do not permit changing the linear interval definition. Report all boundaries, group counts, hit rates, z and contribution magnitudes, positive-score route rates, fallback, context coverage, and empty or weak groups.

CrossCoder does not force decoder columns to have unit norm, so raw z depends on positive rescaling of a feature. For feature i, define the fixed contribution score c_i as z_i times the sum of the decoder-vector L2 norms over the 14 layers. A group's score s_e is the sum of its member contribution scores divided by its total number of members; inactive members contribute zero to this mean. Thus c_i is invariant to a positive rescaling of an individual feature, but remains a normalized decoding-amplitude measure. A/B use exactly the same score formula. Do not select a score using validation or learning results.

Fix the paired seeds to `20261018` and `20261019`. For B, shuffle the same feature pool with that seed and split it according to A's eight group sizes. Save group memberships. Within each pair, all four conditions share expert initialization and data order. Use an independent RNG for the trainable router so that constructing it does not alter expert initialization. Resolve tied maximum scores and all-zero scores with a stateless hash of the seed plus source file, context and absolute token position. Choices must not depend on batch, rank or processing order.

**Gate before formal training:** all eight bins must be nonempty and the frequencies must have a nonzero span. Each expert must receive at least 200 TRAIN positions with a positive score under the actual top-1 routing, covering at least 20 contexts. All-zero fallback traffic cannot satisfy this gate. If the gate fails, record the linear scheme as insufficiently testable under the current data and score definition. Preserve preflight and necessary smoke evidence; mark formal A/B and secondary C/D as `NOT RUN`. Do not change bins or append a logarithmic alternative to replace the primary result.

## Model and training

Use eight independent ordinary FFNs without biases: 1,024 → w → GELU → 1,024. Train every matrix; these are not LoRA adapters. The input x is h centered by the TRAIN mean_h and divided by the TRAIN scalar_rms. All four conditions use the same fixed normalization. Add delta back to the original h, then apply the frozen original final norm and head. Initialize the up projection randomly with scale 1/sqrt(1,024), and initialize the down projection to zero. Initial logits must exactly equal those with a zero branch. Freeze every Base, head and CrossCoder parameter, while preserving autograd from the head to the experts. Zero up-projection and task-router gradients on the first step are expected with zero down projections. On later steps, check gradients for the experts that actually receive inputs and for the router.

Candidate widths are w = 64/128/256. Expert parameter counts are 8 × 2 × 1,024 × w: respectively 1,048,576 / 2,097,152 / 4,194,304. The main top-1 FFN matrix cost per position is approximately 4 × 1,024 × w FLOPs, counting one fused multiply-add as two FLOPs. List GELU and indexing costs separately. The bias-free C/D router adds 8,192 parameters and approximately 16,384 FLOPs per position. Do not claim that all four conditions have exactly equal total parameter counts. List the frozen vocabulary-head projection and teacher costs separately.

Use AdamW with initial learning rate 3e-4, betas (0.9, 0.95), epsilon 1e-8, weight decay 0.01 and global gradient clipping at 1. Set dropout to zero. The effective batch is 256, with no accumulation. The maximum budget is 512 updates = 131,072 valid training targets, approximately one pass over the old windows. Keep data order fixed across conditions. Do not discard overflow tokens because of routing. Carry the tail into the next epoch's permutation under the same seed, preserving 256 valid targets in every update. Use the fixed endpoint, without early stopping or best-checkpoint selection. There are two seeds × four conditions = eight arms. A/B runs in the first four-GPU wave and C/D in the second.

After actual smoke timing, choose the largest candidate width estimated to complete all eight formal arms within 30 minutes of active computation, using memory and throughput only. If 512 updates do not fit the budget, select the largest common budget from 512/256/128 that does fit. Use exactly the same budget for all four conditions and both seeds. If even 128 updates do not fit, record insufficient resources rather than presenting an incomplete run as complete. Freeze the width candidates and budget-selection rule before inspecting learning results. Record the actual selection in the config and supplementary receipt.

For C/D, define f_e on the complete valid global batch as the fraction of positions routed to expert e. Define P_e as the batch mean of expert e's softmax probability. The raw LB term is 8 times the sum over experts of stopgrad(f_e) × P_e; it equals 1 under uniform routing and probabilities. C uses coefficient 0 and D uses 0.01. Record both raw and weighted terms in both conditions. Do not add LB to A/B when it cannot change their fixed routing. Record actual load, target count per expert, gradient/update counts and zero exposure; do not artificially equalize them.

## Evaluation, interpretation and claim boundary

Define the low-frequency evaluation feature set separately as eligible-pool features at or below the 10th percentile of TRAIN frequency. Define high frequency as features at or above the 90th percentile. These are evaluation subsets; **the eight routing bins remain linearly equal-width**. Each mask is the union of positions where at least one corresponding feature is active. Count each position once. Derive these masks from the teacher without the branch inserted. Record mask intersections and coverage. Do not claim that a particular item of low-frequency knowledge has been learned. Estimate a per-feature validation quantity only with support of at least 20; retain `NA` below that threshold. Do not use validation to reselect the feature pool.

The primary metric is the endpoint difference A−B in mean NLL over low-frequency validation positions for each pair. Also report overall and high-frequency differences, per-feature differences, macro feature means, position means and training-budget curves. The prespecified practical margin is δ = 0.005 nats per valid position, approximately a 0.5% relative perplexity difference; utility judgment remains with the researcher. Record support for this branch only if both pairs have low-frequency differences ≤ −δ and each pair's overall difference is ≤ 0.005. Mixed directions, inadequate support or a failed gate yield insufficient evidence. If both low-frequency differences are ≥ δ, record non-support. Consistent directions that do not reach the practical margin also yield insufficient evidence. Two bounded seeds do not guarantee population-level statistical significance.

Report original-Base, initial, intermediate and endpoint losses. Evaluation checkpoints are 0/32/128/256/512, restricted to the actual budget. For every feature and expert, compute q_i,e: the fraction of positions routed to expert e on which feature i is active. Report the nominal frequency-group → actual-expert matrix, out-of-group features, low-frequency coverage, load and zero exposure. Explain that nominal group membership does not guarantee actual delivery to that expert. Greater frequency homogeneity together with improved learning in A establishes co-occurrence only. Greater homogeneity without improvement shows that this property is insufficient. Homogeneous nominal membership without more homogeneous inputs shows that the property was not realized in the inputs. If all conditions perform worse than the Base, retain training/fidelity diagnostics first; do not declare every grouping ineffective.

Deliver A/B learning curves, separate C/D curves, assignment matrices/load and within-expert actual q distributions first. Then report complete singular-value summaries for trained experts' up and down projections separately, for both W and ΔW, with min(w, 1,024) singular values. Display the first 16. Keep absolute values separate from normalized shares. Compare spectral distributions across strategies without treating equal expert indices as functional correspondences. Singular values do not measure directional similarity, and a flat spectrum does not establish capacity.
