# Frequency-interval residual MoE: bounded result

The controlled preflight and engineering Smoke completed, but the formal learning comparison was **NOT RUN**. All eight linear frequency bins were nonempty; the frozen mean-contribution router nevertheless sent zero TRAIN positions to the first three experts. This is insufficient routing coverage, not evidence that frequency grouping has no learning benefit.

The intended question was whether frequency grouping improves NTP loss on inputs containing low-frequency features relative to random grouping with exactly the same eight group sizes. The Base, original norm/head, and final fourteen-layer/F16384 CrossCoder were frozen. Eight independent nonlinear FFNs were inserted at block-28 residual-post, before the original final norm. A/B were the primary fixed-router comparison; C/D were learned-router references differing only in load-balancing loss.

The TRAIN support pool contained 9,615 features. Eight equal-width intervals on the original probability scale had sizes [9253,183,124,34,7,3,2,9]. A's actual top-1 position counts were [0,0,0,129,201,646,9893,119843]. The last expert received 91.69% of TRAIN positions. Both random groupings passed the predeclared per-expert 200-positive-position/20-context gate; A did not. A's routes were identical across the two seeds, with no ties or zero-score fallback; this is not a two-seed learning replication.

Of 95,544 TRAIN positions containing a low-frequency evaluation feature, 88,524 (92.65%) went to the last expert and none to expert zero, where all low-frequency features were listed. Group membership did not imply actual token assignment. Low-frequency inputs covered about 73% of TRAIN and 75% of EVAL; the high-frequency union covered every position. These cohorts overlap and do not directly measure acquisition of rare knowledge.

Native zero-branch logits and loss matched exactly on the 32-position shape-matched guard. Four one-seed, 128-position, 32-update Smoke fits passed gradients, fitting, finite held-out diagnostics, telemetry and serialized next-update recovery. All small TRAIN fits decreased while the separate 128-position validation losses rose slightly. These intentionally repeated-set diagnostics do not rank learning strategies. External-process recovery remains unqualified.

The result narrows the operationalization: the original linear lists plus mean contribution cannot provide adequate learning exposure to all eight experts in this window. The broader learning hypothesis remains open. A surviving rival is the score's dependence on marginal frequency and amplitude. The sole suggested next check, **not executed**, is to keep the exact lists and data and preregister one TRAIN mean-score calibration for both A/B, first testing whether exposure coverage is restored.
