## Knowledge update

The static finding is a common dominant component plus reproducible, distributed domain preferences. Atom24605 accounts for about86%–90% of coefficient-square contribution in the readout domains. Per-token Top-32 sparsity coexists with14,192 atoms used across the full panel. Excluding the common atom,90% of the readout remainder requires700–1,898 atoms. Concentration alone does not locate domain information or functional importance, and atom-index curve shape does not establish a Gaussian law.

The intervention adds a functional link: compared with matched controls, redirecting an update away from enriched decoder spans selectively reduces the associated domain's relative benefit. Full-step and half-step contrasts agree. However, every full-update cross-domain mean remains helpful. The evidence therefore identifies conditional local benefit channels, not destructive interference or an isolation advantage.

## Mechanistic interpretation and speaking narrative

We made the advisor's atom-axis picture measurable, found a shared large component plus weaker reproducible domain preferences, and then changed the parameter that actually generates the observed representation. Redirecting that change supplies a controlled functional test of the enriched directions. The resulting story advances from which atoms are used to which representation changes carry domain-selective benefit. It does not yet explain why the preferences formed during pretraining.

Let \(a\) be native attention output, \(c\) the incoming attention context and \(\Delta W\) the virtual update. Then \(\Delta a=\Delta Wc\). With target loss sensitivity \(g_t=\partial L_t/\partial a\), local help is approximately \(-\langle g_t,\Delta a\rangle\). In plain terms, both where the update moves the representation and how the target responds to that direction matter. Activation magnitude alone specifies neither relationship.

The explicitly post-hoc descriptive [supplement](../../../../../../MoE_Router/experiments/20260926_atom_mechanism/analysis/mechanism_supplementary_readout.json) gives full/half-step first-order mean-level relative absolute errors of0.519%/0.266%, over16 equally weighted source/readout means. These are not sample-level error guarantees. First-order substitution into the unchanged primary formula also approximates measured M. This helps explain the observed small-step response without adding a support gate, proving independent SAE-coefficient mediation or explaining representation formation.
