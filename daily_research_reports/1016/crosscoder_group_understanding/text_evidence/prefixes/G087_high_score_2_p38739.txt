Eating like a Foodie Most of the Time


Yes I am a foodie.
My eyes sparkle at the sight of a homemade dish that I have not yes experienced.
My nose can sniff melted butter from miles away. I don’t eat as much as I want to. I am very careful about what I eat 80% percent of the day so that I can eat what I want to during the rest of the time. I eat lots of whole grains and insane amounts of vegetables.
Here are my foodie rules.

1. I would rather butter over sugar. Butter doesn’t cause me to feel guilty. Sugar makes
me feel hungry and tired.
2. I never eat soup at restaurants unless it is a really good restaurant and I know that they make the soup from scratch.
3. I am slightly lactose intolerant but I will eat any dairy products when out of the house. I will not risk getting a stomach ache for processed cheese or fake milk products. I will risk a stomach ache for risotto any day of the week.
4. I almost never have any cookies in my house unless they are homemade or really good store-bought cookies. There is no way that I would risk my foodie calories for some boring cookie.
5. At home I only drink water.

Note: I try to live by these rules but I tend to break them sometimes. Nobody is perfect.

What are your foodie rules?

This was the result of Austin’s CSE meeting this morning.

I almost feel bad talking about this. I know so many people that would give just about anything to have a supportive, understanding Committee watching over their child. I truly feel fortunate. But his team is just awesome in their devotion to Austin and doing what is BEST and APPROPRIATE for him. Even though we have so many questions, they GET him.

The common theme today was “Austin is very bright, but he is struggling so much, and we don’t know why”. Math is generally his best subject, but we’ve seen the difficulties spread to that subject recently (last nights homework was a horror!). His special ed teacher did a little “study” on Austin yesterday. They were doing a math sheet. It was painful to watch as he struggled she said. After about 10 minutes, she took his pencil away from him and asked him to verbally complete the math sheet. He had the entire sheet done within about 2 minutes.

And THIS is the crux of all his problems. He can verbally do math, and relay stories/ideas, but when he has to translate that to written word/numbers everything grinds to a halt. Half the time his numbers/letters are backwards or the order is flipped. (It is definitely not dyslexia!) He can’t process what he needs to do, in what order, and with what amount of control. And again, some days he’s better than others, and it’s not as much of a chore for him.

It’s like there is a loose wire in his brain… sometimes it connects, other times the connection blinks off and on, and sometimes the connection is just broken.

I want to fix it, his teachers and therapists want to fix it, we just need to know HOW to fix it! I had placed all my faith in the neuropsych being the answer we needed, but it wasn’t. Now I am placing that same unending faith in Boston and hoping they can find the answer.

They have also noticed a lot of stimming. Verbally through humming, rubbing his fingers on objects and tracing circles over and over. The idea of the circles (I’ve seen it too) has me wondering. We’ve been calling these absence seizures. But with a true absence there should be no confusion and no lethargy after it. But everyone is definitely seeing that.
