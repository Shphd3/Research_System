## How understanding changed today

The following is **AI synthesis**, not researcher mechanism judgment.

1. **Present selection and future learning allocation are separate questions.** The fixed-library negative result constrains the tested operationalization, not separate training in general. An existing restricted analytical counterexample shows that allocation can create expert differences absent at initialization. Whether that happens in natural text still requires trajectories. The possibility also cannot turn the current negative result into effective routing.
2. **Retention concerns the complete computation.** Unchanged expert parameters and optimizer state verify that those objects are frozen. They do not preserve old predictions when the shared input path changes. Acquisition, retention on fixed old-domain evaluation sets, and interventions on shared paths therefore require separate readouts. The targeted reading of 17 papers reinforces these conditions; it does not externally confirm the project's mechanism.
3. **Finite resources include update opportunities.** The current contract schedules 4,608 shared-parameter updates and 1,536 updates per private expert. Mixed-batch gradients use global target-count normalization, and learning rate decays with global step. An analytical model with equal conditional step sizes therefore does not directly explain these AdamW trajectories. These are configuration/source facts, not measured optimization outcomes.
4. **Different shared objects and budgets can yield opposite external results.** Always-active common experts, shared trunks updated by every task, and frozen public modules read by new experts play different roles. DeepSeekMoE and OLMoE report opposite directions for shared-expert ablations under their respective contracts. Conditions must remain attached; the label “shared” does not imply universal utility.
