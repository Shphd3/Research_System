You Hear It Often:

Bill Bohn, Associated Financial Group; Mary Curtin, 4Iimprint; and Linda Evans, Douglas Dynamics participate in an Employee Retention Roundtable, co-sponsored by Associated Financial Group and The Business Journal

Staff The Business Journal

The economy is improving and good employees may be thinking it’s time to dust off their resumes. So what does an employer do about it? The panel assembled recently came up with a long list of ideas to engage and — hopefully — retain employees. No matter what the size of your organization, you’ll learn as I did by reading their thoughts.

Panelists: Mary Curtin - 4Imprint, Vice President of Administration

Linda Evans - Douglas Dynamics, Vice President of Human Resources

William Bohn - Associated Financial Group, President and CEO

Moderator: Mark Sabljak, Publisher, The Business Journal Serving Greater Milwaukee

MR. SABLJAK: The goal today is to discuss strategies to maintain employee trust and high morale now and in the future. Recent studies have shown that one out of every three employees is not as engaged as they have been in prior years, and consequently, these employees are looking to leave their employers in 2012. Yet only 9 percent of leaders surveyed expect their turnover to increase in the coming year. So a real gap between understanding of employees looking to leave and their leadership thinking they've got them. Among the reasons cited for the decline of engagement and potential departures from organizations is what employees cite as their treatment during the recession. Are you aware of the sentiment from these studies? If so, are you or other businesses experiencing these trends?

MS. CURTIN: We're aware of the trends and we monitor employee satisfaction within our team. Last year it was about 5 percent in turnover and the prior year it was about 6 percent. For us, one of the key players for our business is inside sales, which can be a stressful position, and we certainly do all we can to retain people. We don't produce any products. So our biggest asset is our team members, and we take care of them because we believe that happy team members will make happier customers.

We participate in the Great Places to Work Survey, and we're going into our fifth year. We look at engagement, and satisfaction with how we're managing the organization to identify trends and potential issues we should be addressing. It's certainly something that we keep an eye on because we don't want to be naive and not be aware of something that we can change.

MS. EVANS: Same thing with Douglas Dynamics. We monitor all employee turnover. As other businesses have been hit in the recession, we haven't experienced that yet. This year will be one of those challenges. We continue with increases in pay but may be more limited in what we have been able to do in the past.
