\begin{abstract}
Sparse mixture-of-experts models often evaluate routing quality through expert
usage counts, but equal counts alone do not define a feature-space partition. We
study a constructive problem: given unlabeled route-relevant features in a
spectrally normalized representation space, can a fixed router dispatch the
feature distribution uniformly across experts without labels or batch-level
capacity forcing? We define uniform feature dispatch as an equal-probability
partition of feature space and introduce Spectral Partition Routing (SPR), a
recursive quantile router whose split directions may be fixed, locality-driven,
or derived from expert-weight spectral prototypes. Quantile thresholds provide
the equal-mass guarantee; split directions determine geometry and expert
alignment. We prove the equal-mass property and validate the construction in
full synthetic runs. SPR reduces held-out LoadCV from 0.086 for random linear
routing to 0.034 on Gaussian and spherical features, and from 0.308 for
prototype-only routing to 0.062 on covariance-flat non-spherical features.
Expert-guided SPR keeps the quantile balance while reaching 0.895
cell-to-prototype alignment and improves a controlled hard-routing task from
12.61 to 9.03 validation MSE. The experiments also expose limits: count balance
does not imply feature specialization, locality-only splits do not reliably
recover latent classes, and naive slow recalibration can destabilize routes.
\end{abstract}
