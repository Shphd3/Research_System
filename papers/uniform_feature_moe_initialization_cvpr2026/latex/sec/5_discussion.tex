\section{Discussion and Limitations}

All positive statements in this paper are controlled sufficient constructions.
The paper does not prove a necessary-and-sufficient theorem for all routers or
all hidden-state distributions. It also does not claim that hidden spectral
uniformity alone implies load uniformity; the covariance-isotropy counterexample
explicitly shows that it does not. Spectral flattening is useful in the
elliptical experiment because the toy data satisfy a strong elliptical
assumption.

Training dynamics are outside the main claim. Static uniform assignment and
training-time preservation are different questions. During optimization, token
features, router rows, and expert matrices can move. Preservation then depends
on margin lower tails and drift budgets, not only on step-zero geometry. The
short-sequence next-token setting is therefore a later nonuniform-spectrum and
margin-preservation problem, not the main evidence for this paper.

\section{Conclusion}

Uniform expert usage under an unconstrained linear top-1 router is not a
consequence of balanced feature frequencies alone. It is a geometric property
of the distribution seen by the router. In the clean controlled case, the
sufficient condition is two-sided: token directions must match the spherical
model, and expert prototypes must induce equal-measure cells on that sphere.
The experiments support this picture and separate it from nearby but weaker
signals. More tokens reduce sampling noise but do not remove a fixed
prototype-cell floor; expert spectral directions can construct centers but do
not guarantee balanced cells; covariance isotropy can coexist with nonuniform
directional support; whitening repairs routing only under the controlled
elliptical assumption; and balanced assignment forces counts by changing the
assignment mechanism.

The resulting claim is intentionally narrow but useful. It gives a baseline for
what uniform feature routing would require before training dynamics enter. The
clean case clarified here tells us what must be checked before load uniformity
can be interpreted as evidence of meaningful routing.
