\chapter{Mean-Free Spectral Regime: Spike--Tail Quantization and Softmax Amplification}
\label{sec:meanfree_spectral_softmax}

In this section we analyze the failure mechanism of low-bit softmax
\emph{after mean bias has been removed}.
Our objective is to isolate the residual source of instability
arising purely from spectral anisotropy,
i.e., the coexistence of low-rank spike directions and a long semantic tail.

Throughout this section, all vectors are assumed to be mean-free in the token dimension.
Thus the rank-one mean component has already been eliminated,
and we focus exclusively on spike--tail structure.

\subsection{Spectral Decomposition of Mean-Free Logits}

Let $z \in \mathbb{R}^H$ denote a pre-softmax logit vector
for a single token and attention head.
Assume $z$ is centered:
\[
\mathbb{E}[z] = 0.
\]

We consider a spectral decomposition
\begin{equation}
z = z_{\mathrm{sp}} + z_{\mathrm{tail}},
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{sp}} = U_k a,
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{tail}} = U_\perp b,
\label{eq:spike_tail_decomp}
\end{equation}
where:

\begin{itemize}
\item $U_k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times k}$ spans the top-$k$ singular directions,
\item $U_\perp$ spans the orthogonal complement,
\item $\mathrm{Cov}(a) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{sp}}$ contains large eigenvalues,
\item $\mathrm{Cov}(b) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{tail}}$ contains small eigenvalues.
\end{itemize}

We assume a typical anisotropic regime:
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{sp}} \gg \lambda_{\mathrm{tail}},
\qquad
k \ll H.
\]

Thus energy concentrates in a low-dimensional spike subspace,
while semantic diversity resides in the long tail.

\subsection{Low-Bit Quantization in the Spike-Dominated Regime}

Consider symmetric blockwise quantization with $b$ bits.
Let $s$ denote the per-block scale determined by a high percentile
of $|z|$:
\[
\Delta = \frac{s}{2^{b-1}-1}.
\]

Although $z$ is mean-free,
its elementwise maxima are typically governed by spike components.
Under mild sub-Gaussian assumptions on $b$,
extreme-value theory yields
\[
\max_j |z_j|
\approx
\max_j |z_{\mathrm{sp},j}|
\quad \text{with high probability.}
\]

Therefore the quantization step satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Delta \propto \|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty.
\label{eq:delta_spike}
\end{equation}

This phenomenon can be described as \emph{spectral scale dominance}:
the quantization grid resolution is calibrated by the spike,
not by tail magnitudes.

\subsection{Tail Signal-to-Noise Collapse}

Under stochastic rounding,
\[
\hat z = z + \varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon]=0,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_j) \le \frac{\Delta^2}{4}.
\]

Using \eqref{eq:delta_spike},
\[
\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_j)
\propto
\|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty^2.
\]

Define a tail coordinate $j$ such that $|z_{\mathrm{tail},j}| \ll \|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty$.
Then its effective signal-to-noise ratio is

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR}_j
=
\frac{|z_{\mathrm{tail},j}|}
{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_j)}}
\approx
\frac{|z_{\mathrm{tail},j}|}{\|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty}.
\label{eq:tail_snr}
\end{equation}

When spike energy dominates,
$\mathrm{SNR}_j \ll 1$ for most tail coordinates.
Thus long-tail semantic information is buried beneath quantization variance,
even though the representation is mean-free.

This mechanism is independent of mean bias.
It arises purely from spectral anisotropy.

\subsection{Softmax Jacobian Amplification}

Softmax with temperature $\tau$ is
\[
y_i = \frac{\exp(z_i/\tau)}{\sum_k \exp(z_k/\tau)}.
\]

Its Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
J(z)
=
\frac{1}{\tau}
\left(\mathrm{diag}(y) - y y^\top\right).
\label{eq:jacobian_softmax}
\end{equation}

First-order propagation gives
\[
\Delta y \approx J(z)\varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathrm{Cov}(\Delta y)
\approx
J(z)\Sigma_\varepsilon J(z)^\top.
\]

In regimes where spike energy induces peaky distributions,
one coordinate $h$ satisfies $y_h \approx 1$.
Then for $i \neq h$,
\[
J_{ih} \approx -\frac{y_i}{\tau}.
\]

Substituting into covariance propagation yields

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(\Delta y_i)
\approx
\frac{1}{\tau^2}
y_i^2
\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_h).
\label{eq:broadcast}
\end{equation}

Thus quantization noise in the dominant spike coordinate
is broadcast to all tail coordinates.
This is a \emph{dense coupling} effect,
arising from the $y y^\top$ term.

\subsection{Hessian-Induced Systematic Drift}

Softmax is nonlinear.
Second-order expansion yields

\[
\mathbb{E}[\hat y - y]
\approx
\frac{1}{2}
\sum_{j,k}
H_{i,jk}(z)
\Sigma_{\varepsilon,jk},
\]

where $H$ is the Hessian tensor.
Because $\Sigma_\varepsilon$ scales with $\|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty^2$,
systematic drift magnitude also scales quadratically with spike dominance.

Thus spike-determined scaling produces both:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Variance amplification (Jacobian),
\item Systematic bias (Hessian curvature).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{High-Dimensional Amplification}

In high dimension,
\[
\|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_2
\sim
\sqrt{k}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
\|z_{\mathrm{tail}}\|_2
\sim
\sqrt{H-k}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{tail}}.
\]

Even when $\sigma_{\mathrm{sp}}$ exceeds $\sigma_{\mathrm{tail}}$
only moderately,
the extremal coordinate magnitude of $z$ scales as

\[
\|z_{\mathrm{sp}}\|_\infty
\sim
\sigma_{\mathrm{sp}}\sqrt{2\log H}.
\]

As $H$ grows,
this extremal growth ensures that quantization scale
is almost surely governed by spike directions.

Thus high dimensionality magnifies spectral imbalance.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Even in the absence of mean bias,
low-bit instability arises from spike--tail anisotropy.
The mechanism proceeds as:

\[
\text{Spike Dominance}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{Scale Inflation}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{Tail SNR Collapse}
\;\rightarrow\;
\text{Softmax Coupled Amplification}.
\]

Therefore,
mean removal alone does not guarantee FP4 stability.
Achieving precision-robust softmax requires
spectral flattening (whitening),
adaptive scaling,
or operator redesign that mitigates spike-induced scale dominance.
